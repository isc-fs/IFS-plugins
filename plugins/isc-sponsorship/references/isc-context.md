# ISC Formula Student Racing Team: context for sponsorship work

Read this before drafting anything a sponsor will see.

## Who we are

ISC Formula Student Racing Team is the official Formula Student team of Universidad
Pontificia Comillas (ICAI), Madrid, Spain. Students design, build, and race a
prototype electric race car each season, and compete internationally against other
university teams.

- Season car naming: `IFSxx` (IFS08 was the 2025/26 car, IFS09 is 2026/27).
- Category: Electric Vehicle (EV), with a Driverless programme.
- Departments: Driverless, Control Electronics, Telemetry, Powertrain, Chassis,
  plus Project Management, Marketing, and Sponsorship on the business side.
- Recent competitions: Formula Student Austria and Formula Student Spain 2026.

## What actually persuades a sponsor

Rank these above generic "support student talent" appeals:

1. **Recruitment pipeline.** Sponsors hire our alumni. ICAI engineering students who
   have shipped a real vehicle are exactly who technical companies want. For many
   sponsors this is the single strongest argument.
2. **Real technical use of their product.** If they make sensors, actuators,
   connectors, PCBs, CAD/CAE software, or test equipment, we will use it in anger and
   give them honest engineering feedback. This is worth more to them than a logo.
3. **Brand visibility with a specific audience.** Competitions, social media, campus
   presence. Be concrete about reach; never invent numbers.
4. **CSR / education narrative.** Real, but weakest on its own. Use as support, not
   as the lead.

## Tone rules

- Concrete over enthusiastic. Engineers read these emails. Specific technical detail
  earns more credibility than adjectives.
- Never invent statistics, follower counts, placement results, or past sponsor names.
  If a number is needed and unknown, flag it for a human to fill in rather than
  guessing.
- Never promise a deliverable that is not in the agreed tier.
- Short. A first-contact email that runs past 200 words does not get read.

## Language

- Spanish-headquartered companies: write outreach and proposals in Spanish.
- International companies: write in English.
- Internal documents, the Notion pipeline, and board-facing summaries: always English.

When in doubt about a company's home country, check their website domain and
headquarters before choosing a language.
